The passage

Every build walks the same four stretches.

The order matters. Skipping the first one is why redesigns get rebuilt.

Stretch one

Understand the constraint

What the business actually runs on, what the team can maintain, and what the current system gets wrong. Before a line of code.

Stretch two

Build the spine

Data model, key flows, and the interface that carries them. Made to be read by whoever inherits it.

Stretch three

Harden and tune

Performance, technical SEO, access control, and patching. Treated as build quality, not as a phase-two invoice.

Stretch four

Hand over well

Documentation, editing setups people can use, and support if the work needs continuity after launch.

Before you write

Magento, Shopify, and the questions that come up first.

Answered plainly, because the shape of a project is usually decided before anyone opens an editor.

What kind of ecommerce development do you take on?

Magento 2 and Shopify are the core of the work: storefront and theme builds, custom extensions and app logic, checkout and conversion paths, catalogue and inventory structure, and the payment and shipping integrations behind them. Alongside that I take on performance tuning, technical SEO, security hardening, platform patching, and production debugging on stores that are already live.

Magento 2 or Shopify — which do you recommend?

It depends on where the complexity sits. Magento 2 earns its overhead when the catalogue, pricing rules, B2B logic, or ERP integration are genuinely complicated and you need control over the data model. Shopify and Shopify Plus win when speed to market, operational simplicity, and a managed checkout matter more than deep customisation. I work across both — Hyva, Luma, Breeze, Liquid, GraphQL, and REST — so the recommendation is not a defence of whichever platform I happen to know.

Can you migrate or replatform an existing store?

Yes. Migrations, redesign rollouts, and replatforming are regular work, including the parts people underestimate: URL and redirect mapping so search rankings survive the move, catalogue and customer data integrity, and keeping the old system running until the new one is genuinely ready.

Do you build anything other than storefronts?

Regularly. ERP modules, inventory management systems, HRM tools, reporting dashboards, role-based admin interfaces, and internal workflow applications — usually for teams replacing spreadsheets or manual process. On the marketing side, CMS-led sites, landing pages, and lead-generation structures built in WordPress, Laravel, Vue, React, or Next.js.

Is technical SEO part of the build, or a separate project?

Part of the build. Crawlability, content hierarchy, internal linking, structured JSON-LD data, image handling, Core Web Vitals, and rendering performance are decided while the architecture is still soft. Retrofitting them onto a finished site costs more and works less well, which is why they are treated as build quality here rather than a phase-two invoice.

How do you handle security and platform patching?

As a standing responsibility, not an add-on. That covers threat modeling, vulnerability scanning, dependency and patch management, access control, session and payload validation, rate limiting, safer admin patterns, and secure CI/CD and code-review practice. Where compliance matters, I work to ISO 27001 and GDPR expectations.

Do you work with existing teams and inherited codebases?

Yes — a good share of the work is inherited systems rather than greenfield builds. That includes joining an existing team, taking over a codebase someone else wrote, and retained support after launch. Handover is treated as a deliverable: documentation, editing setups a non-developer can actually use, and code written to be read by whoever inherits it next.

Where are you based, and do you work with clients elsewhere?

Ahmedabad, India, working with clients worldwide in English. Engagements run as freelance projects, contract builds, retained support, or collaboration inside an existing team.
